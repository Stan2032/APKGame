## [0.12.1] — 2026-08-06
- **The season track is now visible and playable**, not just computed. A track nobody watches filling is the same failure as the daily streak that nothing read.
  - **Season screen**: tier, XP bar, days remaining, and a 50-tier horizontal ladder showing *both* lanes at every tier — the paid lane is displayed whether or not it is owned, because hiding the offer would make the purchase a guess. The ladder auto-scrolls to the player's own tier, since a 50-tier list opened at tier 1 says nothing about where you are.
  - **Home entry** above the shop, showing tier and days left, because the season is the thing that keeps paying and should be what a returning player sees first.
  - **Match summary** shows `+N season XP · tier T` beside the stardust, derived from the repository's own write rather than plumbed back through three record methods.
- `season_pass` wired through `BillingManager` as a **consumable** — deliberately, since it is bought per season and consuming it is what lets the same product be purchased again next season. Consume-then-grant, the same redelivery-safe order as the stardust packs.
- The pass offer hides entirely when billing cannot serve it, matching the shop's posture: no dead buttons before the Console products exist.
- Season state rolls over **on display as well as on write**, so a player returning after a boundary never opens the screen to last season's tier.
- Tests: 145 (90 `:core` + 55 `:app`), 4 new. One of them caught its own bad assumption: it assumed a `seasonId = 0` save was stale, when today's date genuinely falls in season 0 — the test was wrong, not the code.

## [0.12.0] — 2026-08-06
- **Duels normalize: both players are issued every shell and the top preview.** An audit of the monetization question found the game was *already* selling PVP advantage while DESIGN.md claimed it was not — the preview upgrade (✦250/✦600) tripled look-ahead **in duels**, and Breaker's 50 damage against Bolt's 34 sat behind ✦900 of purchasable stardust.
  - The deeper reason this had to change: **this game cannot referee loadouts.** A duel travels as `(seed, turns, hash)` and the receiver re-simulates it, so it can verify the *physics* but never the *shopping* — a claimed loadout is taken on trust, which is the hole that got the Blind gambit cut, except this one decides who wins. With no matchmaking there is no ladder to absorb a paid edge either; it just breaks the match your friend sent you.
  - Normalizing is not a revenue sacrifice. It is precisely what allows the season track to be sold hard without any of it being pay-to-win. (D-29)
- **The season track: 28 days, 50 tiers, fed by verified play.** Campaign clears scaled by difficulty and stars, the daily scaled by streak, code duels win *or* lose, plus a little for kept gambits and met objectives. Hot-seat earns nothing, same as it earns no stardust.
  - Free lane pays stardust, thickening at every 5th and 10th tier. Paid lane (`season_pass`, a consumable bought **per season**) pays shells, trails, preview tiers and larger drops — **additive and retroactive**, so buying at tier 30 immediately pays every premium tier up to 30.
  - **No tier-skip product.** It would convert, and it would also turn the only long-term progression system into a receipt.
- **Why not Clash Royale's card levels**, since that was the ask: CR works because a server owns your stats and trophy matchmaking pairs comparable players. We have neither by design, and stat progression is exactly the thing this architecture cannot verify. The season track delivers the long tail without requiring accounts, a backend, or trusting the sender. (D-30)
- **The tier curve is calibrated, not chosen.** The first attempt needed a committed player **69 days to finish a 28-day season** — a track nobody could complete. `SeasonTest` models a committed day and a tourist day and fails if the season becomes unfinishable *or* trivial; it caught this immediately. At the shipped values a committed player lands around day 23, and the daily alone would take eight months.
- Season state rolls over by date: a new season wipes XP, claims and pass ownership, applied on read as well as write, so a player who was away for a month never sees last season's tier.
- Tests: 141 (90 `:core` + 51 `:app`), 9 new — including the load-bearing one, that nothing on the track can reach a duel: every shell the premium lane grants must already be in the duel loadout, and no granted preview tier may exceed what duels issue.

## [0.10.1] — 2026-08-06
- **Fourth audit pass: DETERMINISM.md contained a trap.** It is the document backing the project's single load-bearing claim, and it had drifted in three ways that mattered:
  - Its list of hashed content stopped at the 0.2.0 protocol — no anchors, no gambit id, no gambit-kept verdict, no objective state, all of which fold in now. That omission is the dangerous kind: this is the doc a contributor reads before adding an input, and **an input that is not hashed is one a sender can edit after the fact without the receiver noticing.** That is precisely how a re-declared gambit would have become free points.
  - It stated playback runs at "2 ticks/frame" — the exact frame-counted pacing that was *removed* in 0.4.0 for running the game at double speed on 120Hz panels. The doc was describing the bug as the contract.
  - It said nothing about generation being part of determinism, though both devices regenerate the system from the seed and the difficulty bands go through `StrictMath.pow`.
- **`DeterminismTest.everyTurnInputFieldChangesTheHash`** now enforces the rule structurally: it asserts `TurnInput`'s exact field list and that changing each one moves the verification hash. Adding a field fails the test with the field list in the message, rather than letting an unhashed input ship quietly.
- TESTING.md listed one core test file when there are eight, and omitted every suite added since 0.4.0. Rewritten against the tree, and the extension rules now carry the lessons that cost measurement to learn: prove a gambit's threshold against real levels, prove an objective is winnable before shipping it, and A/B any performance claim in the same session.
- `GambitTest.kt` had quietly become three unrelated classes; split into `GambitTest`, `ObjectiveTest` and `ShareCodeSizeTest` so the filenames mean what they say.
- Tests: 129 (78 `:core` + 51 `:app`), 1 new.

## [0.10.0] — 2026-08-06
- ⚠️ **Protocol v6.** Systems now carry an objective, which changes both what a seed generates and what winning means, so v5 codes are refused.
- **Capture: hold orbit around the marked world** (VISION Stage C, first objective beyond destruction). Four unbroken seconds inside a ring, and the system is yours.
- **The physics wrote this feature, not the design doc.** VISION specified "achieve a stable orbit"; measuring first showed that is *unreachable by aiming*. A shot arriving from far away is on a hyperbolic trajectory — a two-body encounter with nothing to dissipate energy cannot capture, it must leave. Across twelve generated levels no unbraked shot exceeded **191 ticks** of ring dwell, and every refined optimum pinned itself against the slowest launch the game permits, which is the search asking to go slower than the game allows. The RETRO brake is the only dissipation that exists — and with it the same levels reached **339–818 ticks**. So Capture is an orbital-insertion puzzle: arrive slow, burn at the right moment, drop into the ring. (D-26)
- Consequences that follow from that, rather than from taste:
  - The bar is **240 ticks** — above the 191 a flyby can reach, so a flyby can never satisfy it by accident. Same principle as rejecting levels the obvious shot wins.
  - A Capture system **issues a RETRO shell** whatever the player has unlocked. An objective that demands a tool you may not own is a dead end, and the burn button now follows the shell that actually flies rather than the one selected — gating it on the selection would have left the brake unreachable on precisely the systems that cannot be won without it.
  - **This is the first thing in the game that gives the RETRO shell a reason to exist.**
- Objectives are campaign-only and never assigned without proof: ~28% roll at difficulty ≥2, and only if `proveCapture` finds a real solution — otherwise the system stays a Breach. D-24's rule applied again: never set a task you cannot confirm is achievable. Worst measured generation 280ms (the proof early-exits on first solution). (D-27)
- Holding the ring counts as having worked the field, so a one-shot capture is worth three stars; a hit inside a broken declaration still cannot be a winning shot, for any objective.
- Renderer: the ring is drawn as the band it actually is — inner and outer edge, filling toward gold as the run builds — so the rule is visible rather than explained.
- Tests: 128 (77 `:core` + 51 `:app`), 7 new. One of them pins the finding itself: if an unbraked shot ever holds the ring, Capture has stopped requiring the burn that justifies it and the test fails.
- Trimmed a 56-second test down to 0.98s: it was brute-forcing the proof grid through the full per-tick playback path when it needed exactly one solution.

## [0.9.3] — 2026-08-06
- **CI stopped building everything twice.** With `main` now the default and changes arriving by pull request, a push to the feature branch fired both the `push` and the `pull_request` trigger — two identical ~5-minute builds, on different refs, so the concurrency group could not collapse them. CI now follows the flow it is meant to gate: pull requests verify the proposed change, pushes to `main` verify the merged result, and `workflow_dispatch` covers a branch that has no PR yet. The concurrency group keys on `head_ref || ref_name` so both event types resolve to the same plain branch name and a newer run cancels its predecessor.
- Consequence worth knowing: **the APK/AAB artifacts now come from the run attached to the open PR**, not from a feature-branch push. README, RELEASE.md and the TODO's sideload step updated to say where to click.
- D-20 rewritten now that the default branch actually moved: the repo has a PR flow for the first time (it was born empty, so its first push became both the default *and* the only branch, and a branch cannot open a PR into itself).

## [0.9.2] — 2026-08-06
- **Second audit pass: the share-code size claims were wrong at the top end.** Docs said "~200-byte challenge codes" and "~30 bytes of header + 8 bytes per shot". Measured: the frame is 26 bytes, a turn is 10 (not 8 — v3 added anchors, v5 added the gambit), plus 5 per anchor. A code to *open* a duel is **52 characters**; the longest match the rules allow (30 turns, every anchor spent every turn) is **1039**. The size matters because the code is the netcode — it has to survive a human pasting it into a messenger — so it is now held by `ShareCodeSizeTest` against a 1200-character budget, chosen against the ~1530-character concatenated-SMS ceiling. Every protocol version so far has spent bytes; the headroom is what the next ones may spend.
- Verified and left alone: all five IAP product ids, their grants (600/2200 stardust) and suggested prices match `Economy.Iap` exactly; all three Ad Pass perks are real in code (×2 on campaign/daily/duel payouts, ✦50/day, and every rewarded-ad surface gated behind `!adPassActive`).
- `main` created at the tip of the session branch so the repo can finally have a PR flow — the repo was born empty, so its first push became both the default *and* the only branch, and a branch cannot open a PR into itself. D-20 rewritten to record what actually happened and what remains owner-only (the default-branch switch is a repo setting no agent can make).
- Tests: 121 (71 `:core` + 50 `:app`), 2 new.

## [0.9.1] — 2026-08-06

- Corrected a stale difficulty band in DESIGN.md: it still quoted `[0.03·0.72^d, 14×]`, superseded in 0.7.0. The old floor sat *above* what difficulty 0 actually measures, which is what had been silently pushing early levels onto the fallback path.

## [0.9.0] — 2026-08-06
- ⚠️ **Protocol v5.** A turn now carries its declaration, and that declaration can nullify a hit, so v4 codes are refused rather than re-judged under different rules.
- **Gambits: call your shot** (VISION.md Stage B). Before firing you may declare a constraint — *Featherweight* (under 40% power, ×1.5), *Two-body* (pass through the pull of two different worlds, ×1.8), *Stolen speed* (end faster than you launched, ×1.6), *The long way* (never cross the line to the target, ×2.2). Keep the promise and the shot pays the multiple. Break it and the shot is wasted: the hit does not land. That stake is the point — it turns mastery into something risked rather than something displayed, which is what the word has meant all along.
- **VISION's "Blind" gambit was cut, not implemented.** It was the one proposal the physics cannot see: a peer verifying a share code re-runs the simulation and derives everything else from the flight, but nothing in the inputs records whether the sender looked at their aim preview. A scoring rule that has to be believed, in a game whose whole premise is that nothing is believed — and one paying a multiplier, which is exactly where cheating pays — is a hole. The four that shipped are each decided by the simulation. (D-23)
- **The menu is proved per level and per seat.** The first pass offered all four everywhere; measuring twelve curated levels found *Two-body* had **zero** winning shots on three of them. The game would have been inviting a bet it could not pay. The generator now proves feasibility and offers only what it found — and per seat, because intersecting both seats left half of sampled duels with nothing to declare at all, and the two stations genuinely face different fields. The proof is conservative by construction: every option offered was set by a real simulated hit, so the sweep can under-report but never invent. (D-24)
- **Fixed a design bug found while building it**: a kept declaration multiplied *style points*, which duels never read — duel outcome is HP and nothing else. Declaring in PVP was therefore all risk and no reward. A kept declaration now multiplies **damage**, the currency the mode actually settles in. (D-25)
- **"Stolen speed" was recalibrated from measurement, not feel.** At the first threshold it was satisfied by roughly nine winning shots in ten — a declaration nearly every hit honours is not a stake. Across 201 winning shots on twelve levels the median gain was 101; the threshold moved to 120, near the 80th percentile.
- Three stars now also recognise a kept declaration as having worked the field, and a hit inside a broken declaration can no longer be the winning shot — because it did not land.
- **Proving all this costs nothing.** A dedicated feasibility sweep measured 300–500ms on hard levels. The solver sweep the generator already runs simulates every sample shot to completion and `ShotResult` already carries the measurements, so feasibility now rides along on it for four comparisons per winning sample. The dedicated sweep survives only as a second opinion for seats that came back empty.
- Tests: 116 (66 `:core` + 50 `:app`), 20 new.

## [0.8.0] — 2026-08-06
- **Aiming is a floating joystick.** The stick now appears wherever the finger lands and reads the pull *relative to that point*, instead of being anchored to the station. Anchoring it there meant the gesture lived permanently in one screen corner: the thumb covered the play area it was aiming into, and the reachable arc was clipped by the screen edge — you could not pull "past" the corner, so half the aim space was hard to reach and the other half was hidden under your own hand. Pull back to aim the opposite way; the arrow reads as a slingshot rather than a steering wheel.
  - Built on a hand-rolled `awaitEachGesture` rather than `detectDragGestures`, because the latter only reports its origin *after* touch slop is exceeded — the ring would have appeared a few pixels off the finger and one gesture-slop late. It now centres on the true touch-down and is visible from the first frame.
  - Drawn as a dead-zone ring (so "inside here does nothing" is visible, not folklore), a power arc, the pull line, and a heading spike tinted cyan→yellow with power.
- **Fine-tune buttons repeat on hold.** They were single-tap only, which meant ~450 taps to swing the aim 45°. Press and hold now auto-repeats after 320ms, and the *step itself* grows the longer it is held (×1 → ×3 → ×8, with a haptic tick at each change), so one control covers "a hair to the left" and "swing it right round". A tap is still exactly one step — the immediate step fires on press, not on release, so a tap shorter than a frame still counts.
  - The rewritten control is a raw pointer target, which carries no semantics of its own, so its TalkBack role and single-step activation are now declared explicitly rather than inherited from `TextButton`.
- Portrait control reserve 188dp → 196dp: the fine-tune targets grew to a 48dp minimum (accessibility touch target), and reserved rows are how this layout avoids covering the board.
- Tests: 103 (55 `:core` + 48 `:app`; two new: aim is origin-relative across distant touch points, and the dead zone does not steer while the stick clears on release).

## [0.7.0] — 2026-08-06
- ⚠️ **Protocol v4.** Curated generation and the longer solver horizon change what a seed produces, so older codes are refused rather than replayed into a different universe.
- **Levels now have a purpose.** A player cleared a level with a straight shot, used no gravity, and took three stars — and they were right that the level had none. Measurement confirmed it was systemic: **5 of 36 campaign levels fell to a near-straight shot.** Two root causes, both fixed:
  - The generator scattered bodies with only *separation* rules and never asked whether the direct station-to-station corridor was blocked. Layouts are now built **around the shot they want to force**: stations first, then a **keeper** placed on the direct line and sized to eclipse it, then **shepherds** alternating either side at offsets that make a curved route plausible. Placement with intent, not rejection-sampled randomness.
  - The solver measured *what fraction of the whole aim space connects*, which says nothing about *whether the first thing anyone tries works*. `fallsToNaiveShot` now rejects any level beatable by pointing within 22° of the target at any power — including every fallback path, which was quietly reintroducing the exact bug it was meant to guard against.
  - Result: **0 of 72 levels across six sectors fall to a straight shot**, average 4.7 bodies per level.
- **Three stars now mean "you played the game".** Shot count alone gave full marks for a lucky lob; the third star additionally requires the winning shot to have worked the field — stolen speed from it, curved, or struck a comet.
- Difficulty bands recalibrated: they were tuned for open corridors that no longer exist, and difficulty 0 was landing *below* its own floor, silently pushing early levels onto the fallback path. The solver horizon also rose 600→950 ticks, because curved routes take longer than lobs and genuine solutions were being scored as misses.
- Generation got **~20× faster** (17–40ms, from 300–630ms) as a side effect: curated layouts are accepted on the first attempt instead of burning 24 random ones.

## [0.6.0] — 2026-08-05
- **The difficulty solver stopped lying.** Anchors let players bend the field, so a hit-rate measured on a *fixed* field no longer describes how hard a level is — the game's "measured difficulty" guarantee had quietly become false the moment anchors shipped. `anchorLeverage` now measures the thing that actually matters: **of the shots that currently miss, what fraction can a single anchor rescue?** Systems where anchors trivialize aiming are rejected.
- Corrected a design error of my own along the way: the first version also demanded a *minimum* leverage ("anchors must matter here"), which rejected perfectly good pure-aim layouts — variety in how much anchors help is a feature — and each rejection costs another full solve sweep, tripling generation time. Measure always, reject only trivialization.
- Recorded an honest open question: duel generation measures ~330ms. Stashing the probe showed ~324ms without it, so this is not the probe — duels solve the aim space from both stations and 0.4.0's comets/pulsars added bodies to every sim. Logged as an optimisation target rather than left as folklore.

## [0.5.0] — 2026-08-05
- **Anchors are playable.** Tap "Anchors", then tap the field to place up to three point masses that bend your own shot; tap one to flip it attract↔repel, again to remove. The aim preview updates live, so you *see* the curve you are authoring. This is the inversion VISION.md argues for: the player stops solving a fixed field and starts designing one. Anchors are spent by the shot they shaped, and in duels they travel inside the challenge code — so your opponent replays the arena exactly as you bent it.
- Fixed a bug found while wiring it: replayed opponent shots were not being given their anchors, which would have desynced any anchored duel. Now covered by a test that fires an anchored shot and verifies the emitted code round-trips with its anchors intact.
- Fixed the reported **HUD overlap**: the HUD floated over the canvas, and since the portrait board letterboxes to less than screen height the controls ate into play area. Portrait now reserves rows; the canvas takes what is left.
- Tests: 96.

## [0.4.0] — 2026-08-05
- **Moving bodies.** Comets drift across the field on deterministic rails and pay a bonus if you can lead one; pulsars breathe, their gravity swelling and fading on a fixed period so timing becomes part of the shot. Both are pure functions of the simulation tick, so determinism survives untouched — exactly as the D-22 assessment predicted, and no engine restructuring was needed.
- **Slingshot scoring.** Peak speed is tracked per shot, and velocity stolen from the field (peak minus launch) now pays out. This rewards the game's actual fantasy: whipping around a planet instead of lobbing straight.
- ⚠️ **Replay protocol bumped to v2.** Simulated outcomes changed, so v1 codes are refused rather than silently mis-verified. Nothing has shipped publicly, so no player codes exist in the wild — this is why the break was taken now rather than after launch.
- **Aiming redesigned.** The "∠ 270.0° ⚡ 55.0%" readout read as instrumentation and said nothing about whether a shot would reach; replaced with a filled power meter labelled by feel (Light → Full) and larger, labelled fine-tune controls.
- **Home rebuilt** away from stock Material cards: hairline-bordered slates with drawn orbit/comet/duel/spark glyphs over the live starfield, so the first screen belongs to this game specifically.
- **Copy trimmed throughout** — the UI over-explained itself. Roughly 700 characters removed from Duel, Shop, Settings and Help; mechanisms still named, lectures removed. The stale hardcoded "0.1.0" in About now reads the real build version.
- Tests: 87 (new `MovingBodyTest` covers comet motion, wrapping, pulsar periodicity, slingshot payout and end-to-end verification with drifting bodies).
